Record sounds, place them on a line or a square, and travel between them.

How to do the assignments

parts 3 and 4

Before you startGet your sounds in

  1. Put on headphones. Press Microphone on and allow the microphone.
  2. Press Record a sound, hold a steady vowel or note for 1–3 s, press Stop.
  3. Rename it (click its name). Record a few more: different vowels, different pitches.
  4. The four examples let you try everything before recording; delete them with ×.

Part 3A slider between two sounds

  1. In Line, pick the left and right sounds.
  2. Press and drag the slider. All the way left is the left sound, all the way right is the right sound.
  3. In between you hear a blend: pitch and vowel shape both move gradually.
  4. Press Hold to keep it sounding while you move the slider with the keyboard arrows.

Part 4A square of sounds

  1. Each sound is a dot in the Square. Choose Arrange to drag dots where you want them.
  2. Back in Play, press and drag anywhere. Near a dot you hear mostly that sound; on a dot, its recording.
  3. The lines from your finger to the dots get thicker the more each sound counts.
  4. Try Crossfade at the top to hear the difference between mixing volumes and blending shapes.

For the video, record your screen while you drag across the square.

How the blend works

  • Analysis. Each sound is measured once: its pitch, and the loudness of every harmonic. A line through those harmonic peaks is the sound's shape, its vowel.
  • Weights. On the line, the slider sets how much each end counts. In the square, each dot counts by one over its squared distance from your finger, so the nearest dot wins and on a dot only that dot counts.
  • Spectral blend. The pitch is a weighted average on a musical (log) scale; each new harmonic's loudness is the weighted mix of every sound's shape at that frequency. The result is played with sine waves.
  • On a dot the app fades to the real recording, so you hear exactly what you recorded. Crossfade instead just mixes the recordings' volumes.
